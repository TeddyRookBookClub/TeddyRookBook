### How to approach hard problems

A hard problem rarely gives way to the first thing you try. These habits help:

- **Try small cases.** Work out $n=1,2,3,4$ by hand and look for a pattern before you try to prove anything.
- **Draw it.** A sketch of the board, the circle or the graph often shows the idea before the algebra does.
- **Look for an invariant.** Find something that never changes when you make a move. It can prove that a goal is out of reach.
- **Work backwards.** Start from what you want and ask what the step before it would have to be.
- **Generalize.** Replace a specific number with $n$. The general problem is sometimes easier, and it shows why the answer is what it is.

For a beautiful example of these habits at work, watch 3Blue1Brown's video [The hardest problem on the hardest test](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OkmNXy7er84), about Putnam 1992 A6. When you're ready for real contest problems, the [Putnam solutions guide](/math/competitions/putnam/) links each official problem and gives our own solutions.
