The Continued-Fraction Suite is original music by Teddy Rook: six pieces for piano, each one built from a single number. It began on the Rhythm of Numbers page, where pi is played as a beat, and grew into music you can sit down and play.

How a number becomes music

Any number can be written as a continued fraction: a list of whole numbers that says how to build it, step by step. Pi minus 3 starts 7, 15, 1, 292. The golden ratio is 1, 1, 1, 1, forever.

Each piece reads those whole numbers as repeat counts. A 7 means “seven of these, then one of those.” Bars group into phrases, phrases into sections, and sections into the whole piece, and the list of numbers says how many of each. So every movement has a usual section (marked A in the score), a shorter one that turns up now and then (B), and sometimes a rare block that the number saves for a surprise.

Inside each bar, the same number taps out the rhythm of the left-hand accents. In the score they are the little > marks under the bass notes. The melody walks the number’s decimal digits. You don’t need any of the math to enjoy the music, but it is fun to hear the pattern once you know it is there.
