When a melody moves from one note to the next, it can do one of three simple things:

Step

Go to the very next white key, up or down. C to D is a step. So is G to F.

Skip

Jump over one white key. C to E is a skip (you skip D). So is G to E.

Repeat

Play the same key again. E, E, E.

Bigger jumps happen too, but steps, skips and repeats are most of what your first songs do. In C position, a step uses the next finger and a skip skips a finger.

Steps and skips on the page

In written music, notes sit on lines and in the spaces between them. A step moves from a line to the next space (or a space to the next line). A skip moves from a line to the next line, or a space to the next space. You can see the shape of a melody before you play a note.

Remember

  • Step: the very next key. Skip: jump over one key. Repeat: the same key again.
  • On the page, a step goes line to space; a skip goes line to line or space to space.
