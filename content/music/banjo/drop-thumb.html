The basic bum-ditty plays one melody note every two beats. Fiddle tunes have a note on every beat and often between the beats too. Two thumb techniques fill that gap.

Double thumbing

Instead of strike, brush, thumb, play strike, thumb, strike, thumb: the fingernail on every beat and the thumb on every “and”. The finger can now play a melody note on every beat. The thumb usually stays on the 5th string, which rings like a bell between the melody notes.

Drop-thumb

Now the thumb drops from the 5th string onto the 4th, 3rd or 2nd string to play a melody note on the “and”. The rule that makes it work: the thumb always plays a string below (nearer your face than) the one the finger just struck. Strike the 1st string, drop the thumb to the 2nd; strike the 2nd, drop to the 3rd.

Listen to the second bar: the strikes play E, D, B and the dropped thumb plays B, C, A between them. Every eighth note is now melody, and the hand still moves in the same steady clawhammer arc.

Putting it together

Real tunes mix all of it: bum-ditties, double thumbing, drop-thumbs, hammer-ons and pull-offs. In Soldier’s Joy (clawhammer, open D tuning), the A part is double thumbing on the 5th string; the B part climbs the 1st string with hammer-ons and pull-offs and drops the thumb onto the 2nd string. Try it. Play the drop-thumb exercise slowly until the thumb finds the inside string without your looking. Then learn the A part of Soldier’s Joy two bars at a time, once you have retuned to open D.

Check each one when it feels even at a slow tempo.

  • Double thumbing: the thumb on the 5th string after every strike.
  • Drop-thumb: the thumb drops to an inner string and comes right back.
  • I can play the two bars of Soldier’s Joy in “Putting it together” without stopping.

Remember

  • Double thumbing: strike on every beat, thumb on every “and”.
  • Drop-thumb: the thumb plays the 4th, 3rd or 2nd string, always below the string the finger just struck.
  • Hammer-ons and pull-offs fill the other gaps between beats.
