A lick is a short phrase, usually one bar long, made from a roll plus a left-hand move: a slide, a hammer-on or a pull-off. Licks are the building blocks of three-finger banjo. Learn a few well, and you can play along on tunes you’ve never seen.

The slide lick

The most common lick in bluegrass banjo. Pick the 3rd string at the 2nd fret with your thumb, slide up to the 4th fret, then roll across the open 2nd, 5th and 1st strings. The slide lands on B, a note of the G chord, so it fits almost any bar of G.

Hammer-on and pull-off licks

Two more one-bar licks in G. The first hammers the open 3rd string to the 2nd fret (G to A) and rolls on. The second starts with a pull-off on the 1st string (E to D) and ends with a hammer-on on the 3rd, which leads nicely into the next bar.

A low-string lick

For a deeper sound, let the thumb play on the 4th string: open D, hammered to E at the 2nd fret, then a roll on the open strings above it. This is how a tune’s low part often sounds in three-finger style.

Fill-ins

When the melody holds a long note at the end of a phrase, the banjo doesn’t stop. It fills the space with a roll or a lick on the same chord. Any lick in this chapter can be a fill-in on G; the forward-reverse roll makes a good one too.

Where licks come from

Most licks are passed from player to player, and you’ll hear the same moves in many tunes. Learn them by ear and by tab, then change them a little. That is how every player’s own style begins.

An ending lick

To finish a tune, slow the roll down into a pull-off and close with a pinch: thumb and middle finger together, here on the 4th and 1st strings, both D. Then stop and let it ring.

Put them together

Four bars, four licks, over the chords G, C, D7, G: the slide lick, a forward roll on C, a D7 roll, and the ending. This is the end of the first part of Red Clay Breakdown in miniature.

Try it. Play Boil Them Cabbage Down with rolls, and wherever the melody sits on a G chord for a whole bar, swap in the slide lick. Then try the hammer-on lick in the same place. When you can switch licks without stopping, you’re improvising.

Remember

  • A lick is a short phrase (often one bar) made from a roll plus a slide, hammer-on or pull-off.
  • The slide lick (3rd string, 2nd to 4th fret) fits almost any bar of G.
  • Fill-ins keep the banjo rolling under long melody notes; a pinch ends the tune.
