A major scale is eight notes, from one note up to the same letter an octave higher, in a fixed pattern of steps: whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half. A whole step is two frets; a half step is one.

C major

G major

Start the same pattern of steps on G and you need one sharp, F♯, to keep the last step a half step. On the ukulele the G major scale fits nicely from the open G string. Here is one octave, G up to G, using the open G, then the A and E strings:

Scales and chords

A chord is built from every other note of a scale: from C, take C (1), E (3) and G (5) and you have C major. From the 5th note, G, take G, B and D, and add F, and you have G7. That’s why the chords of a key sound right with the tunes in that key: they come from the same seven notes.

Practice. Play C major up and down three times, evenly, with one finger per fret. Then work out G major in the first seven frets using the board, and play Lightly Row again: its notes are the first five notes of G major.

Remember

  • Major scale: W W H W W W H.
  • C major has no sharps; G major has F♯.
  • Chords are built from every other note of the scale.
