In fingerpicking each finger of the picking hand looks after its own string. The simplest arrangement on the ukulele is one finger per string:

FingerLetterString
thumbp4 (G), and 3 (C) when the root is there
indexi3 (C)
middlem2 (E)
ringa1 (A)

The letters come from the Spanish names classical guitarists use: pulgar, índice, medio, anular. Let the hand hover over the strings with the fingers loosely curled, each over its string. Pluck by curling the finger toward the palm; pluck with the thumb by pushing down and slightly forward.

Three patterns

Hold a C chord and try each pattern. The thumb starts every one; the fingers follow.

In the last pattern, “p” alone means the thumb plays the string that holds the chord’s root: the open G string for a G chord, and the C string (at the 2nd fret, a D) for D7.

A fingerpicked waltz

Island Waltz is a practice tune of our own built on the 3/4 pattern. Play the pattern first while the player picks the tune, then swap.

Practice. Play each pattern slowly on one chord until the notes come out even, with no note louder than the others. Then add a chord change every bar. Keep the hand relaxed; if it tightens, stop and shake it out.

Remember

  • One finger per string: p on 4, i on 3, m on 2, a on 1.
  • The thumb usually starts the pattern, on the chord’s root.
  • Slow and even first; speed comes later.
