The island strum (some teachers call it the calypso strum) fits a great many songs in 4/4. It uses the steady down-up swing from the last chapter, with one strum left out.

Count it

Count1and2and3and4and
Handdown(up, miss)downup(down, miss)updownup
StringsDDUUDU

Say it as “down, down-up, up-down-up.” The miss on beat 3 is what gives it its lilt: the hand swings down past the strings without touching them, then catches them on the way up.

Build it in two halves

If the whole pattern feels tangled, learn the halves first. The first half is down, down-up (beats 1 and 2). The second half is (miss), up-down-up (beats 3 and 4). Loop each half until it’s easy, then join them.

With chord changes

Change chord on beat 1. The up strum on the last “and” can be played as you leave the old chord, with fingers already lifting: it hardly matters what it sounds like, and it gives you time.

Practice. Play the island strum on C alone until you can do it ten times in a row without thinking. Then play along with the C, Am, F, G7 player above. Finally, open Twinkle, Twinkle and strum it with the song.

Remember

  • Down, down-up, (miss), up-down-up: D DU UDU.
  • The hand keeps its steady down-up swing; the miss is on beat 3.
  • Learn it in halves, then join them.
