A barre (say “bar”) is one finger, usually the index, pressed flat across two or more strings at the same fret. You’ve used one already in D7 and B7. Now we’ll use barres on purpose.

How to make a barre

Lay the index finger straight across the strings, just behind the fret, and press with the side of the finger that faces the headstock, which is a little firmer than the soft middle of the pad. Your thumb sits behind the neck, roughly opposite. Use only as much pressure as it takes to make every string ring; if one buzzes, roll the finger slightly or move it closer to the fret. Building a barre takes practice, so work at it in short sessions and rest if your hand gets tired.

B♭: the A shape moved up

Look at the A chord, 2100. Now slide it up one fret, and put a barre at the 1st fret where the open strings were: you get B♭, 3211. The open strings of the A shape have become the barre.

Slide that B♭ shape up one more fret (barre at 2) and it’s B, 4322. Up again, C, 5433. The same shape, wherever it is, is a major chord named after where it sits.

Changing to a barre

Bm turns up in many songs in D and G, and F♯m is its neighbor (2120). In the Canon in D, the chords go D, A, Bm, F♯m, G, D, G, A, one per beat. Practice just Bm to F♯m and back until it flows.

Here the chord changes every bar so you have time; in the Canon it changes every beat.

Practice. Play B♭ and let each string ring, one at a time, adjusting the barre until all four are clear. Then slide the shape up: B, C, C♯, D, naming each one. Finally, play the Canon’s eight chords with four strums each.

Remember

  • A barre: one finger flat across the strings, close to the fret.
  • Move an open shape up and barre the open strings: A → B♭, Am → B♭m, A7 → B♭7.
  • A movable shape is named after where it sits.
