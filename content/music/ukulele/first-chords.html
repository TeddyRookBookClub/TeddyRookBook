A chord is several notes sounding together. On the ukulele you hold a shape with the fretting hand and strum all four strings. The two easiest chords on the instrument each need just one finger.

C major

Ring finger (3) on the 3rd fret of string 1, the A string. Strum all four strings. It sounds bright and settled: a “home” sound.

A minor (Am)

Middle finger (2) on the 2nd fret of string 4, the G string. Strum all four. It sounds softer and a little sad: that’s the sound of a minor chord.

The down strum

Curl your strumming hand loosely, as if holding a small coin between thumb and index finger. Brush down across all four strings with the nail of the index finger, from the G string to the A string, moving from the wrist and forearm, not the shoulder. Some players strum with the pad of the thumb instead, which gives a softer sound; both are fine.

One strum on every beat, four beats per chord. Count “1, 2, 3, 4” out loud and change chord on the next “1.”

Changing between them

C to Am is easy because each chord uses a different finger. While you strum beat 4 of C, get ready: lift the ring finger and set down the middle finger on the 2nd fret of the G string just in time for beat 1. Keep strumming even if the change isn’t perfect yet; the beat matters more than the chord at first.

Practice. Strum C four times and Am four times, around and around, for two minutes, along with the player above or by yourself. Count out loud. When the change feels easy, try two strums of each, then one.

Remember

  • C: finger 3, A string, 3rd fret. Am: finger 2, G string, 2nd fret.
  • Strum down with the index fingernail, from the wrist, over where the neck meets the body.
  • Keep the beat steady and change chord on beat 1.
