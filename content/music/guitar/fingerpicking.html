In fingerpicking each finger of the right hand has its own job. The usual starting plan: the thumb (p) plays the bass strings 6, 5 and 4; the index (i) plays string 3; the middle (m) string 2; the ring (a) string 1.

The hand

Where it rests

The hand hangs over the sound hole, wrist slightly arched, fingers curved. Rest i, m and a on strings 3, 2 and 1 and the thumb on string 6 to feel the starting spot.

The thumb

The thumb pushes down and slightly forward, staying ahead of the fingers so they don’t collide. It moves from the base joint, not by bending the tip.

The fingers

Each finger pulls up into the palm, moving from the knuckle. The hand itself stays still: only the fingers move.

Carcassi’s Prelude in C

A real piece from a guitar method of 1836. Each half bar is one chord: the thumb plays the bass and lets it ring, then i, m and a climb up the top three strings. Hold the whole chord shape down for the half bar.

Try it. Hold C and play p-i-m-a-m-i slowly for a full minute, as evenly as you can, every note the same loudness. Then learn Carcassi’s prelude two bars at a time.

Remember

  • p thumb on the bass; i, m, a on strings 3, 2, 1.
  • Fingers move from the knuckle; the hand stays still.
  • Hold the chord down while you pick it, so it rings.
