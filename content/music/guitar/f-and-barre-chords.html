A barre is one finger, usually finger 1, laid flat across several strings at the same fret. It works like a movable nut. The first barre most players meet is F.

F in three steps

Step 1: Fmaj7

Fingers 3, 2, 1 on strings 4, 3, 2, with string 1 open. A pretty chord on its own, and it fits in place of F in many songs.

Step 2: small F

Lay finger 1 flat across strings 2 and 1 at the 1st fret. Roll it slightly onto its bony side, toward the headstock: the side is harder than the soft middle and presses better.

Step 3: full F

Finger 1 across all six strings at the 1st fret, then the E-major shape in front of it with fingers 2, 3 and 4. Shown below.

Making a barre ring

  • Place the barre finger just behind the fret, straight across.
  • Use the weight of your arm, pulling back slightly, more than thumb squeeze. The thumb sits low behind the neck, about opposite finger 2.
  • Check each string. A dull one usually sits in a crease of the finger: move the finger a little up or down.
  • Short tries, many times. Rest the hand between tries; stop if it hurts.

B minor and B7

Bm is an A-minor shape with a barre at the 2nd fret across strings 5 to 1. If it’s too hard yet, play only the top four strings. B7 needs no barre at all: four fingers, and it’s the chord that leads home to E and Em.

Try it. Each day, play Fmaj7, then the small F, then the full F, strumming once and checking each string. Then play the Em–Bm–C–B7 pattern above with the small Bm if you need to.

Remember

  • F in steps: Fmaj7, small F (xx3211), full F (133211).
  • Barre with the bony side of the finger, using arm weight, not a hard squeeze.
  • Bm x24432; B7 x21202 needs no barre.
