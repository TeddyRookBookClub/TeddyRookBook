A guitar is a wooden box with a long neck and six strings. You press the strings against the neck with one hand and pluck or strum them with the other. Every part has a name, and you will meet these names in every chapter.

The parts

Headstock and tuners

At the top end is the headstock, with six tuning pegs (tuners). Turning a peg makes its string tighter (higher) or looser (lower). Just below it is the nut, a small strip with six grooves that holds the strings in place.

Neck and frets

The neck carries the fretboard, crossed by thin metal bars called frets. Pressing a string just behind a fret makes it shorter, so it sounds higher. Dots on the fretboard (usually at frets 3, 5, 7, 9 and a double dot at 12) help you find your place.

Body, sound hole and bridge

The hollow body makes the sound bigger. The strings cross the sound hole and end at the bridge, where a thin saddle holds them up. You strum or pick over the sound hole.

Here is the neck from the nut to the 12th fret, lying on its side with the headstock to the left. It is drawn the same way tab is written (you will meet tab in chapter 4): the thinnest string, string 1, on top, and the thickest at the bottom. Press any spot to hear it.

How the strings are numbered

Guitarists count the strings from the thinnest one: string 1 is the thin, high-sounding string nearest the floor when you hold the guitar, and string 6 is the thick, low string nearest your chin. Each open string (played without pressing) has a note name. From string 6 to string 1 they are:

E A D G B E, low to high. One old way to remember it: “Eddie Ate Dynamite, Good Bye Eddie.”

The two E strings are both called E: string 6 is two octaves below string 1. When we say “the low E” we mean string 6; “the high E” is string 1.

Try it. Pick up your guitar and find each part named on this page. Then pluck the open strings one at a time from string 6 to string 1, saying their names out loud: E, A, D, G, B, E.

Remember

  • Headstock, tuners, nut, neck, frets, body, sound hole, bridge.
  • String 1 is the thinnest (high E); string 6 is the thickest (low E).
  • The open strings, low to high: E A D G B E.
