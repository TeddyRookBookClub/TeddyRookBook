The full F chord is the open E shape moved up one fret, with finger 1 playing the part of the nut. Slide the whole thing up two more frets and it becomes G; up to the 5th fret and it’s A. The chord takes its name from the note under the barre on the lowest string: the root.

Lift finger 2 (the note on string 3) and the E shape becomes minor, like Em: Fm, Gm, Am. Lift finger 4 instead and it becomes a seventh chord, like E7.

The A shape: root on string 5

The open A shape moves the same way, with finger 1 barring strings 5 to 1 and the other fingers (or a small barre with finger 3) two frets higher. The root is on string 5 (the paler marks above): at the 3rd fret it’s C, at the 5th fret D.

Power chords

Rock and blues players often use just two or three notes: the root and the fifth above it, with no third, so the chord is neither major nor minor. Finger 1 on the root (string 6 or 5), fingers 3 and 4 two frets higher on the next two strings (the fifth, and the root an octave up). Written G5, A5 and so on.

Try it. Play an E-shape barre chord at frets 1, 3, 5, 7 and 8, naming each (F, G, A, B, C). Then do the same with the A shape at frets 2, 3, 5 and 7 (B, C, D, E).

Remember

  • E shape: root on string 6. A shape: root on string 5.
  • The name comes from the note under the barre on the lowest string.
  • Minor: lift the 3rd. Power chords: root and fifth only.
