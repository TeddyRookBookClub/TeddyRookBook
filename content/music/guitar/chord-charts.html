Every chord in this course comes with a small diagram, and so does every chord in the chord finder. Here is how to read them.

What the lines mean

Up and down: strings

Imagine the guitar standing up in front of you, facing you. The six vertical lines are the strings: string 6 (low E) on the left, string 1 (high E) on the right.

Across: frets

The thick line at the top is the nut. The lines below are frets. A dot between two lines means: press there. A number beside the chart (like “5fr”) means the chart starts higher up the neck.

Above the nut

o = play this string open. x = don’t play this string (skip it, or touch it lightly so it stays silent).

Here is E minor, one of the easiest chords: fingers 2 and 3 on the 2nd fret of strings 5 and 4, and every string open around them. Press the chart to hear it. The letters under the strings are the notes it plays.

Finger numbers and x

The number in each dot is the left-hand finger: 1 index, 2 middle, 3 ring, 4 little. In C below, string 6 has an x: start the strum on string 5. In D, strings 6 and 5 are both x: strum only the top four.

Barres and fret numbers

A long bar across several strings is a barre: one finger (usually 1) lies flat across all of them. In the F chord below, finger 1 presses every string at the 1st fret. The second chart has “5fr” beside it: its top line is the 5th fret, so this D chord is played higher up the neck, with a barre at the 5th fret.

Barre chords take practice; they come in chapter 16. For now, just read them.

Written as numbers

Chords are also written as six characters, one per string from string 6 to string 1: 022000 is E minor, x32010 is C, xx0232 is D. You will see this in the chord finder and in many songbooks.

Left-handed? Turn on the left-handed switch near the top of the page and every chart turns into a mirror image, with string 1 on the left: the way your own guitar looks when it stands facing you.
Try it. Without looking at the explanations, read the charts for Em, C and D and finger them on your guitar. Strum each once, only the strings without an x, and check that every note rings.

Remember

  • Vertical lines are strings, low E on the left; horizontal lines are frets.
  • Dots show where to press, with finger numbers; o = open, x = don’t play.
  • A long bar is a barre; “5fr” tells you where the chart starts.
