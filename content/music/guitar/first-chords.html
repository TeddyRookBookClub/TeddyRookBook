A chord is three or more notes played together. On the guitar you hold a shape with the left hand and strum across the strings with the right. These four are the gentlest place to start.

E minor and A minor

E minor (Em): fingers 2 and 3 on the 2nd fret of strings 5 and 4. Strum all six strings. A minor (Am) uses the same two fingers moved over one string (strings 4 and 3), plus finger 1 on string 2 at the 1st fret. Don’t strum string 6.

E major and A major

Add finger 1 to E minor, on string 3 at the 1st fret, and you have E major. For A major, line up fingers 1, 2 and 3 side by side on the 2nd fret of strings 4, 3 and 2; strum from string 5.

Listen to the difference: minor chords sound darker or sadder, major chords brighter. The only change from Em to E is one note, G becoming G♯.

Making every string ring

  • Stay on your fingertips, with the fingers arched, so they don’t lean on the next string.
  • Keep your thumb low behind the neck; that lets the fingers arch.
  • Check one string at a time: pluck each string of the chord slowly. If one is dull, find which finger is touching it and adjust.

Try it. Switch between Em and Am, strumming four times each, for one minute without stopping. Then do the same with E and A. Slow and steady beats fast and patchy.

Remember

  • Em 022000, Am x02210, E 022100, A x02220.
  • Minor sounds darker, major brighter; one note makes the difference.
  • Arched fingertips, low thumb, and check each string.
