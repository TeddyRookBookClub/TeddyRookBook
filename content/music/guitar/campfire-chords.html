These three chords are a family. In the key of G, G is home, C is the step away, and D (or D7) is the chord that pulls you back home. Most simple songs in G use nothing else.

The three chords

G

Finger 2 on string 6 (3rd fret), finger 1 on string 5 (2nd fret), finger 3 on string 1 (3rd fret). Strum all six. Some players use fingers 3, 2 and 4 instead, which makes the move to C easier later; either is fine.

C

A staircase: finger 3 on string 5 (3rd fret), finger 2 on string 4 (2nd fret), finger 1 on string 2 (1st fret). Strum from string 5.

D

A small triangle on the top strings: finger 1 on string 3 (2nd fret), finger 3 on string 2 (3rd fret), finger 2 on string 1 (2nd fret). Strum only the top four strings.

Seventh chords: D7 and G7

A seventh chord adds one more note that makes the chord lean forward, wanting to move on. D7 pulls strongly toward G; G7 pulls toward C.

Listen: play D7, then G. The second chord sounds like arriving home.

Shortcut chords

If the full G is hard at first, play only the top four strings with one finger: string 1 at the 3rd fret, strings 2, 3 and 4 open. It’s a real G chord, just smaller. Move to the full shape when you can.

Try it. Strum G, C, D7, G, four beats each, ten times through without stopping. Then play Night Herd Waltz with the waltz strum from chapter 11 (or just one strum per bar for now).

Remember

  • G 320003, C x32010, D xx0232, D7 xx0212, G7 320001.
  • In the key of G: G is home, C steps away, D or D7 leads home.
  • A small one-finger G is fine to start.
