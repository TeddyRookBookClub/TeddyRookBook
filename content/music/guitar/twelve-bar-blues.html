A twelve-bar blues uses three chords, built on the 1st, 4th and 5th notes of the key. In E those are E, A and B, usually played as seventh chords. The order of the twelve bars hardly ever changes, so once you know it you can join in with any blues.

The pattern

bar 1bar 2bar 3bar 4
Line 1EEEE
Line 2AAEE
Line 3B7AEE (B7)

The last bar often switches to B7 to send you back to the start: that bar is called the turnaround.

The seventh chords

Strum them through the whole pattern with the blues shuffle strum (down-up, long-short):

Swing: long-short

Blues eighth notes are not even. The first of each pair is longer, the second shorter, like a heartbeat or a horse’s gallop: DAH-da DAH-da. That’s called swing or a shuffle feel. The players on this page swing automatically.

The two-string shuffle

The classic blues guitar rhythm part uses only two strings: the open bass string and the string next to it. For E, play strings 6 and 5 together: string 6 open, string 5 pressed at the 2nd fret (finger 1), then at the 4th fret (finger 3), rocking back and forth, two strums each:

For A, move the same shape over one string: strings 5 and 4. Rest the side of your picking hand lightly on the strings near the bridge for a chunky, muted sound (that’s palm muting).

Front Porch Blues

All twelve bars of the shuffle, with a little walk down the 2nd string into B7 in the last bar.

Try it. Play the twelve bars with plain down strums, saying the bar numbers. Then play the shuffle along with Front Porch Blues at 70%, and finally at full speed.

Remember

  • E E E E | A A E E | B7 A E E (or B7).
  • Swing the eighths: long-short.
  • The shuffle: two strings, frets 2 and 4 rocking over an open bass.
