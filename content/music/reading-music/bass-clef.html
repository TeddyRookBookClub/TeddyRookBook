Low notes would need lots of extra lines below the treble staff, so piano music uses a second clef for them: the bass clef (say it like “base”).

The F clef

The bass clef is also called the F clef. It started as a letter F. It begins on the fourth line from the bottom, and its two dots sit just above and just below that line. That line is F: the F just below middle C (F3).

The lines and spaces

From the bottom up, the bass lines are G B D F A, and the spaces are A C E G. Careful: these are not the same as the treble clef’s. The same spot on the staff means a different note in each clef.

As with the treble clef, the sayings are only aids. Good landmarks in the bass clef: F on line 4 (between the clef’s dots), the C in the second space (C3, an octave below middle C, where your left little finger sits in C position) and middle C just above the staff.

Practice

Remember

  • The bass clef is the F clef: its dots hug line 4, which is F.
  • Lines G B D F A; spaces A C E G.
  • The same spot means a different note in the treble and bass clefs. Always check the clef.
