Music moves to a steady beat, the pulse you might tap your foot to. Each kind of note lasts a certain number of beats. In most music you’ll start with, the quarter note gets one beat, so that’s how we’ll count here. (The next chapter shows how the time signature sets the beat.)

The notes, from long to short

NoteLooks likeBeats
Whole notean open oval, no stem4
Half notean open oval with a stem2
Quarter notea filled-in oval with a stem1
Eighth notea quarter note with one flag; groups are joined by one beam½
Sixteenth notetwo flags, or two beams¼

Each note is half as long as the one above it. So one whole note lasts as long as two half notes, or four quarter notes, or eight eighth notes, or sixteen sixteenth notes. Listen: each line below takes the same amount of time.

A stem can point up or down. On a single line of music, notes below the middle line usually have stems going up on the right, and notes on or above it have stems going down on the left. Which way it points doesn’t change how long the note lasts.

Rests: counted silence

A rest tells you to stay silent for a number of beats. Every note value has a matching rest. You still count the beats of a rest, you just don’t play.

Dots

A dot after a note makes it longer by half its own value. A half note lasts 2 beats, so a dotted half note lasts 2 + 1 = 3 beats. A dotted quarter note lasts 1 + ½ = 1½ beats, and it’s often followed by an eighth note to finish the second beat.

Ties

A tie is a curved line joining two notes of the same pitch. Play the first note and hold it through the second, without playing it again: the lengths add up. Ties let a note last across a bar line, or make lengths no single note can show.

Triplets

Sometimes a beat is split into three equal notes instead of two. Three eighth notes played in the time of two are a triplet, marked with a small 3 over or under the group. Count them “1-trip-let, 2-trip-let.”

Remember

  • Whole 4, half 2, quarter 1, eighth ½, sixteenth ¼ (when the quarter note gets the beat).
  • Every note has a matching rest. Count rests too.
  • A dot adds half the note’s value. A tie joins two notes of the same pitch into one longer sound.
  • A triplet fits three notes into the time of two.
