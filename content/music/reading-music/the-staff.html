A staff is a set of five lines with four spaces between them. (Some books call it a stave.) Notes are drawn on the lines or in the spaces, and that position tells you which note to play.

Lines

We count the lines from the bottom up: the bottom line is line 1, the top line is line 5. A note “on a line” has the line running right through its middle.

Spaces

The four spaces between the lines are counted from the bottom up too: space 1 is between lines 1 and 2. A note “in a space” fills the gap and touches the lines above and below it.

Higher on the page, higher in sound

The higher a note sits on the staff, the higher it sounds, and the further to the right its key is on the piano. Each move from a line to the very next space (or a space to the next line) is one step: one white key. Listen to these notes climb the staff, one step at a time.

Music is read from left to right, like words. The notes on the left are played first. Here is the start of Mary Had a Little Lamb: you can see the tune step down, step back up, and repeat, before you play a note.

Remember

  • A staff has five lines and four spaces, counted from the bottom.
  • A note sits on a line (line through the middle) or in a space.
  • Higher on the staff means a higher sound. Line to next space is one step.
  • Read from left to right.
