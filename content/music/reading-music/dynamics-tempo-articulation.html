Most of these marks are Italian words or short forms of them; Italian has been the usual language for musical directions for centuries. You only need a handful to read most music.

Dynamics: loud and soft

Dynamics are written below the staff (between the staves in piano music), in bold italic letters:

SignItalianMeaning
pppianissimovery soft
ppianosoft
mpmezzo pianomedium soft
mfmezzo fortemedium loud
fforteloud
fffortissimovery loud

The piano’s full name, pianoforte, means “soft-loud,” because it could play both, depending on how you press the keys.

Gradual changes are written as words or as long, thin wedges called hairpins. Crescendo (cresc., or a wedge opening to the right) means get louder little by little. Diminuendo or decrescendo (dim., decresc., or a wedge closing to the right) means get softer.

Tempo: fast and slow

A word above the first bar sets the speed. These are the most common, from slow to fast. The beats per minute are only rough guides; musicians and editions differ.

WordMeaningRoughly (beats per minute)
Largobroad, very slow40–60
Adagioslow, at ease66–76
Andanteat a walking pace76–108
Moderatomoderate108–120
Allegroquick and bright120–156
Prestovery fast168–200

Many pieces also print a metronome mark such as ♩ = 100: one hundred quarter-note beats per minute. The metronome shows the tempo word for any speed you choose.

Changes of speed: ritardando (rit.) or rallentando (rall.) means gradually slower; accelerando (accel.) gradually faster; a tempo means go back to the speed you had before. A fermata (a curve with a dot under it) over a note means hold it longer than its written value, for as long as feels right.

Articulation: how each note is played

Legato

Smooth and connected, each note flowing into the next. A curved line over or under different notes, a slur, asks for legato.

Staccato

A dot above or below the note: make it short and detached, with a little silence after it.

Accent

A sideways wedge >: play that note with more weight, louder than the notes around it.

Tenuto

A short line: hold the note for its full value, often leaning on it slightly.

Remember

  • Dynamics: pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff; cresc. louder, dim. softer.
  • Tempo words set the speed: Largo, Adagio, Andante, Moderato, Allegro, Presto. rit. slows, a tempo returns.
  • Legato (slur) smooth; staccato (dot) short; accent (>) stronger; tenuto (line) full length; fermata: hold.
