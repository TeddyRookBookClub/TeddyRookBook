After World War II, the United States and the Soviet Union were rivals in what is called the Cold War. Both built rockets that could carry weapons, and both wanted to be first into space. Each side saw its space program as a sign of its strength. The contest drove a burst of science and engineering, and in twelve years it went from a beeping metal ball in orbit to people walking on the Moon.

Firsts, year by year

Each line marks who did it: USSR for the Soviet Union, USA for the United States.

  • Oct 4, 1957USSRSputnik 1, the first artificial satellite. It circled the Earth about every 98 minutes and sent out radio beeps anyone could pick up.
  • Nov 3, 1957USSRSputnik 2 carries the first animal into orbit, a dog named Laika.
  • Dec 6, 1957USAThe first try to launch a U.S. satellite, on a Vanguard rocket, ends when the rocket explodes on the launch pad.
  • Jan 31, 1958USAExplorer 1, the first U.S. satellite, launched by a team led by Wernher von Braun. Its instruments found the Van Allen radiation belts around the Earth.
  • Sep 2, 1958USAThe National Defense Education Act pays for loans, scholarships and teaching in science, math and languages.
  • Oct 1, 1958USANASA opens, created by an act of Congress signed that July.
  • Sep 13, 1959USSRLuna 2 is the first spacecraft to reach the Moon, by striking its surface.
  • Apr 12, 1961USSRYuri Gagarin, on Vostok 1, is the first person in space and the first to orbit the Earth.
  • May 5, 1961USAAlan Shepard, on Freedom 7, is the first American in space, on a 15-minute flight up and back down.
  • May 25, 1961USAPresident Kennedy asks Congress to commit to landing a person on the Moon and returning safely before the decade is out.
  • Feb 20, 1962USAJohn Glenn is the first American to orbit the Earth.
  • Jun 16, 1963USSRValentina Tereshkova is the first woman in space.
  • Mar 18, 1965USSRAlexei Leonov makes the first spacewalk.
  • Dec 15, 1965USAGemini 6 and Gemini 7 make the first meeting of two crewed spacecraft in orbit.
  • Mar 16, 1966USAGemini 8 makes the first docking of two spacecraft.
  • Jan 27, 1967USAThree astronauts, Gus Grissom, Ed White and Roger Chaffee, die in a fire during a launch pad test of Apollo 1.
  • Apr 1967USSRVladimir Komarov dies when Soyuz 1 crashes on landing, the first death during a spaceflight.
  • Dec 24, 1968USAApollo 8 carries the first people around the Moon.
  • Jul 20, 1969USAApollo 11: Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin are the first people to walk on the Moon, while Michael Collins orbits above.

What it meant in Coalwood

Sputnik went up when Homer Hickam was fourteen. In the years the Rocket Boys were in high school, the country put new money and attention into science and math teaching. At the 1960 National Science Fair, Homer hoped to meet Wernher von Braun, whose team had launched Explorer 1; the two looked for each other there and missed. Homer later spent his NASA career at the Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Alabama, the center von Braun led in the 1960s.

After the race

The two countries went on to work together in space. In July 1975, an American Apollo and a Soviet Soyuz docked in orbit, and their crews met. Some date the end of the race to the Moon landing in 1969, others to this meeting in 1975. People still weigh what each side won and what it cost, and they see it in different ways.
