### The biggest atoms

In Bohr's model the size of an electron's orbit grows as the square of its level number $n$. On Earth, atoms bump into each other long before an electron can sit in a very high level. Between the stars the gas is so thin that it can. Astronomers have detected carbon atoms with the outer electron near level 1,009. Such an atom is about a tenth of a millimeter across, a million times the size of an ordinary atom.

### An orbit with harmonics

An electron that far out behaves almost like a tiny planet. It goes round 6.4 million times a second. When it drops by one level, it gives off radio waves at very nearly that frequency. When it drops by $k$ levels, the frequency is very nearly $k$ times as much. Those are harmonics, the same whole-number ladder as a string's.

The signal that was detected, near 26 MHz, comes from drops of four levels. It is the fourth harmonic of the electron's orbit, picked up by a dish on Earth.

### Where the music stops

For small $n$ the jumps are nothing like harmonics, which is why the Music, Math and Chemistry region says hydrogen does not play music. The harmonic ladder appears only as the atom grows large and starts to act like ordinary moving objects. Bohr called this the correspondence principle.
