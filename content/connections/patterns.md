### Stars from polygons

For more than a thousand years, artists across the Islamic world have covered walls, ceilings and doors with interlaced stars. Many of these designs can be rebuilt from a hidden grid of ordinary polygons. In the 1920s the British scientist E. H. Hankin, studying patterns in India, described a simple way to do it, often called **polygons in contact**:

1. Start with a tiling, say octagons and squares.
2. From the middle of every edge, draw two lines that cross each other at the edge, each leaning in at the same angle.
3. Stop each line where it meets a line from the next edge. Then erase the tiles.

Change the one angle and the whole design changes, from fat flowers to thin, sharp stars. Octagons give eight-pointed stars, and 12-sided tiles give twelve-pointed ones.
