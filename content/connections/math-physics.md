### Three laws

Kepler worked for years on Tycho Brahe's measurements of Mars. No combination of circles would fit them. An ellipse did. He published two laws in 1609 and a third in 1619:

1. A planet moves on an ellipse with the Sun at one focus.
2. The line from the Sun to the planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, so the planet moves fastest when it is closest.
3. The square of the time for one orbit is proportional to the cube of the orbit's size: $T^2 \propto a^3$.

[[viz:ph-ellipse|An orbit with the Sun at one focus. Each colored wedge is swept in the same time, and all the wedges have the same area. Make the orbit more stretched and watch the planet rush past the Sun.]]

### From a curve to a cause

Kepler's laws describe. They do not explain. In 1687 Isaac Newton showed that all three follow from one force that weakens with the square of the distance. The second law is the conservation of angular momentum from the Physics region. The first and third are special to the inverse-square force.

### Real orbits are nearly circles

The Earth's orbit is an ellipse, but a very round one: its eccentricity is 0.017, so the Sun is off center by less than 2 percent. Mars, at 0.093, was just stretched enough for Kepler to catch it.
