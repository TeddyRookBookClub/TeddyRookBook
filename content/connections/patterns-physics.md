### Young's experiment

In the first years of the 1800s the English doctor Thomas Young let light pass through two narrow openings close together and fall on a screen. He saw a row of bright and dark bands. Where the two waves arrive in step they add and the screen is bright. Where one arrives half a wave behind the other they cancel and the screen is dark. Particles would not do that. Waves do.

[[viz:ph-slits|Waves spread from two slits and overlap. Bright stripes are where the waves arrive in step. Move the slits apart or change the color and the stripes move.]]

### Reading the pattern

The spacing of the stripes depends on only three things: the wavelength of the light, the distance between the slits, and the distance to the screen. Measure the stripes and you have measured the wavelength, a length of about half a thousandth of a millimeter, with a ruler.

This is the same adding of waves as the beats in the Music and Physics region. There the waves differ in pitch and the pattern is in time. Here they differ in path and the pattern is in space.

> **Good to know.** The experiment still works when the light is so dim that it arrives one particle at a time. Each particle lands in one spot, and the stripes build up dot by dot. It has been done with electrons and even with large molecules.
