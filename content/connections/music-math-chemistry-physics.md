### The second law, in steps

Kepler's second law says the line from the Sun to a planet sweeps area at a steady rate. That rate is the planet's angular momentum divided by twice its mass. Bohr's rule for the atom says the electron's angular momentum is a whole number $n$ times a fixed amount. Put the two together: the electron in orbit $n$ sweeps area at exactly $n$ times one fixed rate. A planet may sweep at any rate. An electron may not. Kepler's second law is the thing Bohr made come in whole steps.

### Ellipses with whole-number shapes

In 1916 Arnold Sommerfeld let the electron move on Kepler ellipses as well as circles. For level $n$ he found $n$ allowed orbits, all with the same long axis. Their short axes are $k/n$ of the long one, for $k = 1, 2, \ldots, n$. So the shapes of the orbits are fractions: at level 3 the short axis is one third, two thirds or all of the long axis.

### Shapes of their own

Two orbits at different levels have the same shape when their fractions are equal: 1/2 at level 2 is the same shape as 2/4 at level 4. So the shapes that are new at level $n$ are the fractions $k/n$ in lowest terms. Count them over many levels and a familiar number appears: the share of orbits with a shape of their own tends to $6/\pi^2$, about 61 percent. That is the chance that two whole numbers share no factor, the same constant that counts the trees you can see in the orchard of the Rhythm of Numbers. (This small result is new in [the paper](/connections/rhythm-of-numbers/paper/), Section 12.8.)

### What lasted

Ten years later quantum mechanics replaced the little ellipses with standing waves. But the count survived. The $n$ shapes of level $n$ became the $n$ kinds of orbital (s, p, d, f and so on), and the equal energies are the hidden symmetry of the Math, Chemistry and Physics region.

Whole-number ratios of lengths are also where music began, with the string ratios in the Music and Math region. Here the same kind of ratio fixes the shape of an orbit.
