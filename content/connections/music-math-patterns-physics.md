### A triangle that turns

Jupiter overtakes Saturn about every twenty years. Each meeting falls about two thirds of the way round the sky from the one before, so three meetings in a row make a triangle. On 19 July 1595 Johannes Kepler drew this for his class, and the drawing set him on the path to his first book.

The triangle does not quite close. Each time round it has turned a little, and after about 883 years it has turned onto itself.

[[viz:ph-trigon|The meetings of two planets, marked on a circle and joined in order. Jupiter and Saturn make a slowly turning triangle. Venus and the Earth make a five-pointed star every eight years.]]

### The figure is a fraction

Each meeting lands a fixed step further round the circle. The simple fractions closest to that step are the figures: 2/3 is a triangle, 3/5 is a five-pointed star. Write the step as a continued fraction and the next term tells you how slowly the figure turns. For Jupiter and Saturn that term is 14, which gives the 883 years.

### Kepler's music, tested

Kepler believed the planets sang. He compared each planet's fastest and slowest speeds and heard a musical interval in each ratio. Tested against random ratios, his intervals for single planets do no better than chance. His intervals for pairs of the six planets he knew do better than chance, about 1 in 30. But Uranus and Neptune, found after he died, do not continue the pattern.

He was wrong about the music and right that the sky holds figures made of whole numbers.
