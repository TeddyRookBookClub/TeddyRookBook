### Four blocks

The periodic table has a strange shape: tall towers on the sides, a dip in the middle and two loose rows below. Color each element by the kind of subshell its newest electron goes into, and the shape falls into four neat **blocks**:

- the **s block**, 2 columns wide, on the left (plus helium);
- the **p block**, 6 wide, on the right;
- the **d block**, 10 wide, in the middle (the transition metals);
- the **f block**, 14 wide, usually printed underneath so the table fits on a page.

Elements in the same column have their outer electrons arranged alike, which is why they behave alike. Sodium and potassium both have one lonely outer s electron, and both react fiercely with water.
