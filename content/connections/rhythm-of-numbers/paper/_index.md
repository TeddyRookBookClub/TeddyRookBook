abstract:
  - "This work grows out of one guess: that pi is close to 3 + k/(7k + 1) for a suitable whole number k. The guess is right at k = 16, where it gives 355/113, the fraction Zu Chongzhi recorded in the fifth century AD, and it is right for a reason: k/(7k + 1) is a continued fraction cut off after two steps. We follow that reason in three directions."
  - "**Rhythm.** Playing a number as a beat gives a Sturmian word, and we prove that its blocks nest exactly as the continued fraction says: the blocks at each level are as long as the denominators of the convergents. For pi this gives bars of 7 beats, phrases of 106 and 113, and one short phrase that begins at beat 32,996 and closes a block of 33,102 beats. Reading the levels as bar, phrase, section and movement turns a continued fraction into musical form; Teddy Rook used it to compose the Continued-Fraction Suite for piano."
  - "**Primes.** The same machine builds Euclidean rhythms, tunings, mode-locking staircases and a classical factoring method, and coprimality ties them to 6/π². Two open threads get real computations. For prime convergents we derive a heuristic: a typical number has about 2 ln 2 · ln N ≈ 1.3863 ln N prime denominators among its first N convergents. A sample of 400 random numbers fits it, and pi, with 9 prime denominators among its first 5,000 convergents against 10.9 expected, is typical. For the Montgomery–Dyson connection we place a crystal, the rhythm of pi, 5,000 zeta zeros, random matrices, the primes and random points on one scale of rigidity. The picture organizes the mystery; it proves nothing about the Riemann hypothesis."
  - "**Self-similarity.** A continued fraction contains a copy of itself after every term, the idea behind Ford circles, the Stern–Brocot tree, Minkowski's question-mark function, the devil's staircase, Cantor dust (dimension 0.531280506277), the Mandelbrot set's Farey bulbs and the Fibonacci word fractal. Pi's first 10,000 terms pass a chi-square test against the Gauss–Kuzmin law (p = 0.33)."
  - "**Open questions.** Going back to six open questions, we expand pi to 12 million checked terms, test its fractions for primes up to the 20,000th, and prove that for a random number the expected count of prime denominators among the first N convergents is exactly 2 ln 2 · ln N plus a bounded error. A second look (added 2026-11-06) proves the same statement for almost every single number, with the constant 2 ln 2; for pi itself it is still open. A third look (added 2026-11-10) searches pi's convergents to fraction 50,000 and reports five new probable primes, four prime denominators, at fractions 25,332, 27,651, 32,394 and 37,721, and a prime numerator at 42,037: numbers of 13,017 to 21,667 digits found by Teddy Rook, with a proof of the first in progress. Following his notes, it also settles e's convergents modulo primes and the growth of the correlation matrix's largest and smallest eigenvalues, and proves that its spectrum has a limiting shape."
  - "**Physics and astronomy** (added 2026-11-10). A further chapter, from Teddy Rook’s notes, asks where these structures appear in both the very small and the very large. The inverse-square law gives a planet and Bohr’s electron the same law of periods, and its hidden symmetry gives the shell sizes 2n². The continued fraction of a ratio of orbital periods detects resonance by a large term: Jupiter’s locked moons and two chains of exoplanets stand out, while the planets of the solar system taken together do not. The inverse of the correlation matrix has the sign pattern of Liouville’s function, so its two extreme eigenvalues are leading values of non-negative matrices built from common multiples; their ratio is computed to 30 million and whether it has a limit is open. The energy bands of an electron on a lattice in a magnetic field split in the same counts as a number’s rhythm. Kepler’s figure of the meetings of Jupiter and Saturn is read as a continued fraction, and his planetary harmonies are tested against chance."
  - "**The five-subject diagram** (added 2026-11-11). The Connections diagram that this work grew from now has physics as a fifth subject, and 31 regions. Rebuilding it gave two small results. In Sommerfeld’s atom of 1916 the share of orbits whose shape is new at their level tends to 6/π², the same constant as the chance that two whole numbers share no factor. And in the standard map, the model of a regularly kicked wheel, the smooth curves that belong to the Suite’s five numbers break in the order pi, e, the fifth, √2, the golden ratio, with pi’s far ahead of the rest. The second is a computation, with estimates only for the first three."
  - "**Newton** (added 2026-11-11). A chapter on Newton reads the law of gravity the same way. Only the inverse square closes every orbit; under any other power the orbit is a rosette whose flowers are the convergents of one number, and the slow turning of Mercury’s orbit weighs the exponent to seven decimal places. The Metonic and Saros cycles are convergents, and the Gregorian leap-year rule is not. The spring and neap tides are a beat. The period of a long planetary inequality is bracketed by the next denominator of a continued fraction, and a 200,000-year integration of a model solar system finds the great inequality of Jupiter and Saturn, the near-resonance of Uranus and Neptune and a three-planet inequality of Mars with no list of what to expect. It also corrects a statement of the previous chapter about a period of 883 years."
  - "An audit sorts every idea into classical, specialist, new presentation or new. The mathematics is classical or known; the musical-form reading of whole continued fractions, the Suite, the prime-convergent experiment and the spacing spectrum are what we did not find elsewhere. Every number in the text was computed by the scripts in the appendices."
