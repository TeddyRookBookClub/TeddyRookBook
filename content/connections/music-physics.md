### Pressure that rises and falls

A vibrating string pushes on the air next to it, and that air pushes on the air beyond. The push travels outward at about 343 meters a second. Nothing travels with it except the pattern of squeezing and stretching. When the pattern repeats 440 times a second we hear the A above middle C.

### Two waves, added

When two sounds reach your ear at once, their pressures add. If two notes are almost the same pitch, the waves start in step and reinforce each other. A moment later they are out of step and cancel. Then they are in step again. You hear one note that swells and fades. Those swells are called beats, and they come at a rate equal to the difference of the two frequencies: 440 and 443 together beat three times a second.

[[viz:ph-beats|Two notes and their sum. Slide the second note toward the first and the beats slow down. When they stop, the notes match. This is how piano tuners work.]]

### Resonance

Push a child on a swing in time with the swing and a small push each time builds a big motion. Instruments do the same thing with air and wood. The body of a guitar is shaped so that it resonates with the strings and passes their motion to much more air than a bare string could move. Resonance is the reason instruments are loud, and in other regions of this oval it is the reason moons keep time and rings have gaps.
