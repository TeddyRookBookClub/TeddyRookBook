### Missing asteroids

In 1866 the American astronomer Daniel Kirkwood noticed that the asteroid belt has gaps. Almost no asteroids go round the Sun in one third of Jupiter's period, or two fifths, three sevenths or one half. An asteroid there would meet Jupiter at the same points of its orbit again and again. The small pulls would add up, like pushes on a swing, until the orbit changed.

Kepler's third law turns each of those period ratios into a distance from the Sun: 2.50, 2.82, 2.96 and 3.28 times the Earth's distance. Those are the gaps.

### An edge in Saturn's rings

A ring particle that went round Saturn twice for every turn of the moon Mimas would sit at the outer edge of the bright B ring, where the dark Cassini Division begins. The match is within about 50 kilometers once Saturn's flattened shape is taken into account.

### Lock or empty?

So a simple ratio sometimes locks an orbit, as with Jupiter's moons, and sometimes clears it out. A theorem of Kolmogorov, Arnold and Moser says which orbits are safe: the ones whose ratio is hard to approximate by fractions. The pattern in the rings is a picture of which numbers are close to fractions and which are not.

> **This is art.** The sound in the picture is only the two rhythms played as clicks. Nothing in space makes a sound.
