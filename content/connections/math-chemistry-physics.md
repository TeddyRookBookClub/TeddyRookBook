### An extra thing that is conserved

Any force that points toward a center conserves energy and angular momentum. A force that weakens exactly as the square of the distance conserves one thing more: the direction of the long axis of the orbit. That is why a planet's ellipse stays put instead of slowly swinging round like a spirograph.

### The same symmetry in the atom

The electric force in a hydrogen atom is also inverse-square. Wolfgang Pauli in 1926 and Vladimir Fock in 1935 showed that the extra conserved quantity gives the atom a symmetry larger than ordinary rotation. Because of it, all the states of level $n$ have the same energy, whatever their shape, and there are exactly $n^2$ of them. Each holds two electrons with opposite spins. So level $n$ holds $2n^2$: 2, 8, 18, 32.

[[viz:ph-shells|Each box is one state. With hydrogen's symmetry, every state of a level has the same energy. Switch to a many-electron atom and the levels split by shape, and the rows of the periodic table appear: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32.]]

### The symmetry, broken

In an atom with many electrons, the inner electrons hide part of the nucleus from the outer ones. The force is no longer exactly inverse-square, and the symmetry is broken. States of the same level but different shape now have different energies, and they fill in a new order. The rows of the periodic table then have lengths 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32. Each shell size after the first appears twice.
