### Four against two against one

Io goes round Jupiter in 1.77 days, Europa in 3.55 and Ganymede in 7.15. Each period is almost exactly twice the one before. While Ganymede goes round once, Europa goes round twice and Io four times. Pierre-Simon Laplace showed that the three are locked together: the small amounts by which the two pairs miss an exact 2 to 1 are equal, so the whole pattern repeats.

### A test for locked pairs

Two bodies whose periods are near a simple ratio meet in the same places again and again, and the repeated pull can lock the ratio. To find such pairs, write the ratio of the two periods as a continued fraction. If the pair is locked to a simple fraction, the term right after that fraction is large. For Europa and Io the ratio is 2.0073, and the term after 2 is 137. For Pluto and Neptune, locked at 3 to 2, the term is 53.

### What passes and what does not

By this test Jupiter's moons stand far beyond chance, and so do the planets of the stars TRAPPIST-1 and HD 110067. The planets of our own solar system, taken together, do not: random ratios would look as tidy about one time in seven. Not every neat ratio in the sky is a law.
