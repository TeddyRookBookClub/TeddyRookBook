### A swing that keeps its books

Pull a pendulum to one side and let go. At the top of its swing it is high and, for an instant, still. At the bottom it is low and fast. Physics describes this with two kinds of energy: energy of height and energy of motion. As the pendulum falls, the first turns into the second. As it rises, the second turns back into the first. Add them at any moment and you get the same total.

[[viz:ph-pendulum|A pendulum and its two kinds of energy. With no friction the total bar never changes. Turn friction on and the missing energy has gone into heat.]]

### Three things that are kept

Energy is one conserved quantity. Momentum (mass times velocity) is another: when two skaters push apart, what one gains the other loses. Angular momentum is a third: a spinning skater who pulls in her arms spins faster, because the amount of spin has to stay the same.

### Why anything is conserved

In 1918 the mathematician Emmy Noether proved that each conservation law comes from a symmetry. Energy is conserved because the laws of physics are the same today as yesterday. Momentum is conserved because they are the same here as over there. Angular momentum is conserved because they are the same in every direction. So the oldest idea in the Patterns oval, symmetry, is also the deepest idea in this one. The other regions of this oval show where it leads.

> **Good to know.** A pendulum's swing time depends on its length and on gravity, and hardly at all on how wide it swings. Galileo noticed this, and Christiaan Huygens built the first pendulum clock on it in 1656.
