### A pattern of steps

On a piano, the distance from one key to the very next one, black or white, is a **half step**. Two half steps make a **whole step**. A scale is a set of notes chosen by a pattern of these steps. The major scale goes whole, whole, half, whole, whole, whole, half, and lands on the same letter one octave up.

Start the same pattern on any of the 12 keys and you get a major scale in a new key. The notes change, but the pattern of steps (and the feel of the tune) stays the same. That is why a song can be sung higher or lower and still be the same song.

Five-note **pentatonic** scales, with no half steps at all, turn up in folk music around the world. Try playing only the black keys: they form one.
