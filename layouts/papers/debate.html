The Ratification Debate, Side by Side

From September 1787 to May 1790, Americans argued in newspapers, pamphlets and thirteen state conventions over whether to adopt the Constitution. Here the two sides answer each other subject by subject, followed by a timeline of how each state decided.

Ratification by state

Article VII said the Constitution would take effect among the states that ratified once nine had done so. Each state held a special convention, elected for the purpose. The new Congress first met in March 1789 and George Washington took office on April 30, 1789, while North Carolina and Rhode Island were still outside the Union.
