How to read an auction
  • Offered is the amount Treasury set out to sell. Bids is how much investors asked to buy. Over-subscribed by is the difference.
  • Bid-to-cover = bids ÷ amount sold. Higher means more demand. It’s most useful compared with recent auctions of the same security, so the arrow shows the change against the previous six.
  • Tail vs. stop-through: traders compare the auction’s high yield with the “when-issued” yield trading just before the 1 p.m. deadline. Treasury doesn’t publish that figure, so this page compares with Treasury’s end-of-day yield for the same maturity. That’s a rough guide: positive means the auction cleared at a higher yield than the market (a tail, weaker); negative means lower (a stop-through, stronger).
  • Who bought: indirect bidders (often foreign official buyers), direct bidders (U.S. institutions) and primary dealers, who are obliged to bid and absorb what others don’t take.
  • Demand combines bid-to-cover versus recent auctions and the yield versus the market. It is a simple rule of thumb, not an official rating.
