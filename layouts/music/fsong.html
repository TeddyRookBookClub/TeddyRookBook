How to practice with it

  1. Listen first. Press Play and follow along. The note being played lights up in the tab, in the notation and on the fretboard{{ if $s.chords }}, and the chord being played lights up in the row of chord diagrams{{ end }}.
    2. {{- if $s.chords }}
  2. Learn the chords. Press each diagram to hear it, then practice changing between them slowly{{ if $s.pattern }}, before you add the {{ if or (eq $inst "banjo") (in (slice "travis" "pima" "pima34" "forward" "backward" "alternating" "forwardreverse" "pinch") ($s.pattern | default "")) }}picking{{ else }}strum{{ end }} pattern under the tab{{ end }}.
    3. {{- end }}
  3. Slow it down. Use − and + to set the tempo. 60% is a good place to start; the notes keep their pitch.
    4. {{- if $s.pattern }}
  4. One part at a time. Choose Tab only to hear the tune alone, or the other part alone, then put them together.
    5. {{- end }}
  5. Loop the hard part. Under More…, turn on Loop, pick the bars, and play them a few times in a row. Count-in gives you a bar of clicks before it starts.

Click a bar in the music to start from there. Space plays and pauses. Left-handed? Turn on the left-handed view: the chord diagrams and the fretboard are mirrored to match your instrument. Tab reads the same either way: the top line is always the first (highest) string.
