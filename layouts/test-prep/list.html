How to read the maps

  • Unit names are the College Board's own, from each course's public page on AP Central. Everything else is ours.
  • A topic marked Being written is planned on its ladder, but its lesson or practice isn't ready yet. Practice sets skip it.
  • Some units list topics from a college course, like Calculus 3. Those topics go further than AP needs; the notes say so.
  • We don't predict AP scores. The maps show where to learn and practice each unit.

How practice works here

  1. Practice one area at a time. Pick an area of a test, like Advanced Math on the SAT. You see the steps after each question, so you learn as you go.
  2. Then try a timed mixed set. Questions from every area, at the real section's pace. Take the full length or a shorter set. The timer hides with one tap.
  3. Answer the way the test asks. Four choices on all three tests today, plus typed answers on the SAT. On the CLT, every problem is meant to be done by hand. The older paper SAT and legacy ACT have their own sets, with their own timing and rules.
  4. Read your results. Right and wrong by area, your time on each question, the steps for every miss, and the Math Ladder topics to work on, which you can add to your reviews.

The problems come from Math Ladder's own generators, so every set is new. A set shows which topics to work on. It doesn't predict a score, and nothing here does.

Have the tests changed, and did they get easier?

Two of the three changed in the last few years. Each is shorter now, with more time for each question. Whether that makes them easier is argued: the makers say scores are comparable, and some critics disagree. Each page shows the evidence, who says what, and lets you practice both versions.
