❓ How to get the most out of the flashcards
  1. Say the answer before you flip. Trying to remember, even when you can’t, is what makes a word stick. Then flip and grade yourself honestly.
  2. Grade each card. Again brings it back in a few cards. Hard, Good and Easy push it further out (the button shows when you’ll see it next). Cards you know well come back after days, then weeks; cards you miss come back soon. This is spaced repetition.
  3. Little and often. Ten minutes a day beats an hour once a week. Smart review shows the cards that are due first, then a few new ones (you choose how many per day).
  4. Start with the most common words. The words are ranked by how often they occur in the four Gospels: the top 100 make up about 70% of the text, the top 600 about 90%.
  5. Then drill the forms. Word forms shows a real form from the Gospels (say, ἐλάλησεν) and asks what it is; Build the form gives the dictionary word and a description (“aorist active indicative, 3rd singular”) and asks you to produce it. Narrow either one to a tense, mood, voice, person, case or number to practise one pattern at a time, then mix them.
  6. Both languages: choose Greek + Latin and each vocabulary card becomes a three-sided prism. Tap to turn it: Greek, Latin and English, in the order you set. Only words with a clear match between the two languages are included.
  7. Every form is real. Forms come from the Greek New Testament and the Latin Vulgate, with an example phrase and reference, so you only learn forms that actually occur.
  8. Keys: Space or Enter flips; 1–4 grade (Again, Hard, Good, Easy); P plays the word aloud.
  9. Progress is saved only in this browser on this device.
