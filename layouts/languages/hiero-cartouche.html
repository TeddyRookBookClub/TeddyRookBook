A modern game, played with real signs. Egyptians wrote only royal names in cartouches, and they spelled sounds differently from English: they wrote consonants and few vowels, had no letters for l or v, and had several sounds English lacks. When scribes of the Ptolemaic and Roman periods had to write Greek and Latin names (Ptolemy, Cleopatra, Caesar), they spelled them out with sound signs, as this tool does. So there is no single right way to write your name: below, each letter shows the choice made and the other signs you could use.

Read a real cartouche

These are the names of real rulers as they appear in their cartouches. Try to read each one with the sign list, then tap “Show the reading”. Watch for the sun disk 𓇳 of the god Ra: it is written first out of respect but often read last.

Royal names and readings follow Gardiner, Egyptian Grammar, the Excursus on the royal titulary, and Wikipedia’s articles on each ruler. Learn the five royal names in The Five Royal Names and Reading Royal Names (Middle Egyptian I), or Cartouches for young explorers.
