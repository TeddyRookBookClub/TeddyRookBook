❓ How the drills work
  1. Choose a language. Latin mixes the Vulgate Gospels with Caesar, Cicero, Virgil and Ovid. Greek mixes the Greek Gospels with Herodotus and Homer. Both includes everything; use the On switches under Languages & order to turn Latin or Greek off. Tap Change above the sentence to switch at any time.
  2. Press Play. Each sentence plays in the order shown under Languages & order. Use ▲▼ to reorder.
  3. Text / Audio switches choose, per language, whether you see it, hear it, both, or neither. Turn off English text to test yourself.
  4. Say it back. Repeat the sentence out loud during the pause. Pause between sentences sets that gap (1–5 s), and Repeat plays the Latin and Greek up to three times.
  5. Speed runs from 0.5× to 3.5×. It applies to all audio.
  6. Pause to study. While paused, tap any Latin or Greek word. The Word study panel shows its dictionary form, meaning, case/tense/mood and job in the sentence. Tick Word-by-word glosses to see a meaning under every word.
  7. ▶ next to a line plays just that language. ⏮ ⏭ move between sentences. On a keyboard: Space pauses, ← → go back and forward.
  8. What to practice narrows the sentences to the Gospels (one Gospel or one passage), to all the classical authors, or to one work.
  9. Classical sentences were chosen so that each one adds words, verb forms (tense, mood, voice, person) or noun forms you haven't met yet in the set. Their English comes from public-domain translations (several of them the original Loeb translations), so it is sometimes freer or more old-fashioned than a word-for-word rendering.
  10. Red letters mark the words of Jesus in the Gospels. Untick the box to turn them off.
  11. Progress counts sentences in this visit and in total. It is saved only on this device.
  12. About the audio: Gospel Greek is a human reading (Marilyn Phemister). Everything else uses your device’s built-in voices, which differ between devices: Latin uses an Italian voice, and classical Greek uses a modern Greek voice, so its pronunciation is modern, not ancient. If you hear nothing, check that your device isn’t muted.
