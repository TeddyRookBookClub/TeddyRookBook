Languages

Κοινή & Latina

Learn Koine Greek and Latin from the Gospels and the classics. Koine is the everyday Greek of the New Testament, spoken around the Mediterranean from about 300 BC to AD 300; Latin is the language of Rome. Hear each sentence in English, Greek and Latin, then tap any word to see what it means and why it has that form. These two have the most here: a study companion, flashcards, readers, sentence drills and word trees.

Two far older scripts have their own tools and courses. Egyptian hieroglyphs: a sign list, flashcards, a cartouche maker and a typing helper, with two courses in reading Middle Egyptian on the Egyptology Ladder. Linear B, the oldest written Greek: Mycenaean Greek from about 1400 to 1200 BC, a thousand years before Koine, written in syllable signs on clay tablets that record the palaces' goods. Here are the story of how it was deciphered, the signs, flashcards and real tablets to read, with a course on the Ancient Scripts Ladder that also covers Linear A, which no one can read yet.
