Good to know

  • Hex, binary, octal and decimal give each letter its ASCII code, the number a computer stores for it. Capital A is 65 in decimal, 41 in hex and 01000001 in binary. Learn how they work on the Hexadecimal and Binary page or teach them step by step.
  • Morse code is written with dots and dashes, a space between letters and a slash between words. Hear it on the Morse code course.
  • ASL fingerspelling spells English words letter by letter with the hand. It’s only one part of American Sign Language, which has its own signs and grammar. Practice reading it on the fingerspelling page. Handshape drawings: public domain, by Paul Sherman (wpclipart.com), via Wikimedia Commons.
  • Latin words here have no long marks (macrons), the way Romans wrote them. For reading and grammar, see Languages.
