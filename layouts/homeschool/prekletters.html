Pre-K at home · Ages 3 to 5, with a grown-up

Pre-reading games

Letters, rhymes, claps and listening: the games that come before reading. A few minutes at a time, side by side.

For the grown-up

How to play

  • Sit together and play one game, or part of one. Five to ten minutes is plenty.
  • Say the words with your child. Tap a picture to hear its name again.
  • A wrong tap just fades away. Nothing is timed, and there is no score to lose.
  • The voice says whole words, letter names and sentences, never single letter sounds. Turn it off any time and read the words yourself.

Away from the screen

  • Letters: point out letters on cereal boxes, signs and books. Start with the letters in your child's name.
  • Big and small: make letter pairs from paper or magnets, and match them on the fridge.
  • Rhymes: read nursery rhymes and stop before the rhyming word. Make up silly rhymes: “cat, hat, zat!”
  • Claps: clap the parts of everyone's names at dinner: Ma-ri-a, Ben.
  • Same start: gather toys that start the same, like a bear, a ball and a block.
  • Listening: say a short sentence and put down a button or a block for each word.

What comes next

When your child knows many letters and enjoys these games, move on to Phonics Practice, Step 1: Letters and sounds, beside Hannah Ward's lessons. There you say each letter's sound yourself, with a key picture to help.

Hannah's Little House Pre-K and Crystal and Shell Pre-Reading Workbook fit well alongside these games.

The six games

  • 🔠 Big and smallMatch a capital letter to its small letter, and back again.
  • 🔍 Find the letterFind every copy of one letter in a grid.
  • 🎶 Rhyme timeWhich picture rhymes with the first one?
  • 👏 Clap the partsSay a word, clap its parts, and count the claps.
  • 🖼️ Same startPictures whose names start the same, and ones that don't.
  • 👂 Listen for wordsHear a sentence: find the word in it, or count its words.
