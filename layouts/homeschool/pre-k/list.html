Pre-K at home

Pre-K is mostly stories, songs, play and talk, done together. Here is a gentle rhythm for the day, lessons to follow if you want them, games and printables for a few minutes of practice, and a week of ideas to print.

A gentle daily rhythm

Not a timetable: a few good things most days, in whatever order suits your home. Short and cheerful beats long and tired.

  1. 📖

    Story

    Read aloud every day, and more than once if your child asks. Talk about the pictures. Let your child finish a rhyme or turn the page.

  2. 🎵

    Song or music

    Sing nursery songs, clap along, march and freeze. Tap a pot with a wooden spoon for a drum.

  3. 🔢

    Counting

    Count everyday things: stairs, spoons, buttons, snacks. Line up toys from small to big, and look for circles and squares around the house.

  4. 🌳

    Outdoor time

    Walk, run, dig and look. Collect leaves, watch birds and clouds, and talk about what you see.

  5. 🖍️

    Art

    Crayons, play dough, paper and glue. Let your child make whatever they like, then tell you about it.

  6. ✏️

    A little practice

    Five to ten minutes, sitting together: a lesson from Hannah Ward's Little House Pre-K, a Math Ladder lesson, a pre-reading game or a printable sheet. Stop while it's still fun.

Books to read aloud

  • Aesop's Fables: very short stories with a lesson. Read one, then ask what the animal should have done.
  • Winnie-the-Pooh: gentle, funny chapters to read in short sittings.
  • Classic fairy tales: read them first yourself and choose the ones that suit your child.
  • McGuffey's Primer: short picture lessons to read aloud now, and for your child to read later.
  • Hannah Ward's book lists: picture books and read-alouds by age.
  • Nursery rhymes, picture books from your library, and the same favorite book again and again.

For grown-ups

Read, talk, play

  • Read aloud every day. Pediatricians encourage shared reading from birth through at least kindergarten.
  • Talk with your child all day: name things, ask questions, and wait for answers.
  • Play is learning. Blocks, dough, sand, water and pretend play all count.
  • Count everyday things out loud, and let your child count with you.

Screens, kept small

  • The American Academy of Pediatrics suggests no more than about an hour a day of screen media for children 2 to 5, and good programs only.
  • Use the games here together, for a few minutes, and then do the same thing with real things: blocks, spoons, crayons.
  • Most of what's on this page needs no screen at all.

Gently does it

  • Every child learns at their own pace. Follow your child's interest and come back to things later.
  • Praise effort: “You kept trying!”
  • Stop while it's still fun, and try again another day.
