Ray's Arithmetic

The arithmetic books generations of American children learned from, now free to read and download, with step-by-step teaching guides for the first two books.

Start here

For a young child, begin with Ray's New Primary Arithmetic. Every lesson has a guide that tells you what to get out, what to say, and which page of the book to open. Most take about fifteen minutes.

Older children, or those who have finished the Primary book, go on to Ray's New Intellectual Arithmetic: mental arithmetic, fractions, ratio and percentage, also with a guide for every lesson.

Primary course → Intellectual course →
  1. Pick a lesson (start with Lesson 1).
  2. Press Teach this lesson.
  3. Follow the steps one screen at a time, with the book page beside you.

What is Ray's Arithmetic?

Joseph Ray (1807–1855) grew up on a farm in what is now West Virginia, studied medicine, and then spent his life teaching mathematics in Cincinnati, at Woodward High School and Woodward College. His first arithmetic came out in 1834. Over the next decades it grew into a series, from a first book for small children to a higher arithmetic for older students, and it became one of the most widely used schoolbooks in America, alongside McGuffey's Readers from the same Cincinnati publishers.

After Ray's death the series was revised and reissued in 1877 as Ray's New Arithmetics, the edition most homeschoolers use today, and the one on this page. The method is simple and thorough: count real things first, learn each table by heart, then use it straight away in short story problems, and explain every answer in a full sentence. Old prices and measures aside, the arithmetic is the same today.

About these copies

Every book here was published in the United States before 1930, so it is in the public domain there: free to read, print and share. The scans are kept by the Internet Archive, which shows them on this page and supplies the PDF downloads. The teaching guides are written for this site. Modern reprints are also sold, if you prefer a clean printed copy.
