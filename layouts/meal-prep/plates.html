Simple plates, all day

A piece of meat or fish, a starch and a vegetable: the plainest kind of meal, and one of the most useful. Some actors and athletes eat this way several times a day when building muscle; one widely reported example is the plan Dwayne Johnson described for film roles (sources at the end of this page). Each plate on its own is a good, simple meal for kids and families, and they are easy to cook in batches for the week.

🧒 Kids’ portions

Children eat the same plates, just less of them. Start small, about half an adult plate for school-age kids and less for little ones, and let them ask for more. Appetites go up and down from day to day, and that’s normal.

Each plate’s recipe page has its own kids’ note: how big a kids’ plate is and what children can help with.

  • Cut meat into small pieces and flake fish (check for bones) for young children.
  • Cut grapes and other small round foods into small pieces for children under 4.
  • Keep food plain on the side if a child likes foods not touching; it’s the same meal.
  • No honey for babies under 1 year.

📦 Batch-cook a week of plates

One afternoon of cooking can fill the refrigerator with plates for lunches and dinners. The cooling and storage times are USDA’s.

  1. Pick two or three plates your family likes, such as the chicken and sweet potato sheet pan, the beef and pepper rice and the cod with asparagus. Add their recipes to the meal planner for one shopping list.
  2. Cook the starches in big batches. A large pot of rice or quinoa, a tray of sweet potatoes, a pan of baked potatoes. They take the longest and keep well.
  3. Roast proteins and vegetables on sheet pans at the same time. Cook every protein to its safe temperature: poultry 165°F, ground meat 160°F, steaks and fish 145°F (with a 3-minute rest for steaks).
  4. Cool it quickly. Divide food into shallow containers so it cools fast, and get everything into the refrigerator within 2 hours of cooking (1 hour if it’s hotter than 90°F out). It’s fine to refrigerate food while it’s still warm.
  5. Pack plates and label them with the date. Keep the refrigerator at 40°F or below.
  6. Eat refrigerated plates within 3 to 4 days. For the rest of the week, freeze plates right away; frozen leftovers keep their best quality for about 3 to 4 months.
  7. Thaw and reheat safely. Thaw frozen plates in the refrigerator overnight, or in the microwave. Reheat until steaming hot, 165°F, stirring once or twice in the microwave.
A week, made simpleCook on Sunday: plates for Monday to Wednesday go in the refrigerator, plates for Thursday and Friday go in the freezer. Move Thursday’s plates to the refrigerator on Wednesday night to thaw.

📰 The reported plan behind the idea

In 2015, Muscle & Fitness magazine published a day of eating that Dwayne Johnson described, much like the diet he followed for the film Hercules, and many outlets reported it. It was seven meals spread through the day, almost all of them a protein, a starch and a vegetable. The versions differ by film and year: a 2014 version for Hercules reported by Men’s Health used chicken, halibut, egg whites, white rice, baked potatoes, oatmeal, broccoli and asparagus. Published estimates range from about 4,100 (E! Online) to 5,200 calories (FiveThirtyEight, counting everything). Either way, it is an example for a very large, very active adult training hard for a role, about twice what most adults need. It isn’t a target for most people, and it isn’t for children. The simple-plate idea is what carries over to a family kitchen.
