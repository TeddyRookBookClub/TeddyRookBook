🎨 Frost and decorate, simply

Frost a layer cake

  1. Cool completely. Warm cake melts frosting. Trim a domed top flat with a serrated knife if you like.
  2. Anchor it. Dab frosting on the plate and set the bottom layer on it. Slide strips of parchment under the edges to keep the plate clean.
  3. Fill. Spread about ¾ cup of frosting over the bottom layer. Set the top layer on it upside down, so the flat bottom is on top.
  4. Crumb coat. Spread a very thin layer over the top and sides. It’s fine if crumbs show. Refrigerate 20 minutes.
  5. Final coat. Pile frosting on top and push it out to the edges and down the sides. Smooth with an offset spatula or a butter knife, or make swirls with the back of a spoon. Pull out the parchment.
A piping bag from a zip-top bagSpoon frosting into a sturdy zip-top bag, push it into one corner, twist the top and snip off a small corner. Squeeze from the top. Great for cupcake swirls, dots and writing names.
ColorGel food coloring gives bright colors with a drop or two. Start small; colors deepen as they sit.
Easy decorationsA ring of berries, a border of sprinkles pressed onto the sides, chocolate chips in the shape of the age, or a paper stencil dusted with powdered sugar or cocoa.
Let the kids decorateFrost the cupcakes and set out bowls of sprinkles and fruit. Each guest decorates one; it doubles as a party activity.
CandlesA grown-up lights the candles and stays close. Keep hair, sleeves and paper decorations back, and blow them out and take them off before cutting.
Cutting a round cakeFor thinner party slices, cut a circle about 2 inches in from the edge, slice the outer ring into 1-inch slices, then cut the inner circle into wedges.

🥚 Allergy-friendly swaps

Ask families about food allergies when you send invitations, and plan at least one cake everyone can eat. Packaged foods must list the nine major allergens: milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans and sesame. Ingredients change, so read every label, every time (FDA). Bakery and unpackaged foods may not be labeled; ask.

Swaps for the cakes and frosting
AvoidingTry this
Eggs and milkThe egg-free, dairy-free chocolate cake has neither (it does have wheat). Doubled, it makes about 20 cupcakes.
MilkMake the chocolate cake or cupcakes with a plant milk. For frosting, use vegetable shortening or a plant-based butter stick and plant milk. Check labels: some margarines contain milk.
Wheat or glutenA cup-for-cup gluten-free flour blend that contains xanthan gum works in the vanilla cake and cupcakes. Use clean pans and utensils.
Peanuts and tree nutsEvery cake and frosting here is made without nuts. Check chocolate chips, sprinkles and candies for nuts or “may contain” warnings, and leave pecans off the sheet cake.
SesameHummus contains sesame (tahini). Use ranch or another dip, and check bread and bun labels.

Keep the allergy-safe cake on its own plate with its own knife, and serve it first. Label dishes on the table so guests and parents can check.

🌡 Party food safety

On the table (USDA)

  • Food sits out for no more than 2 hours. After that, refrigerate it or throw it away. The planner shows the time.
  • Keep hot food at 140°F or warmer (a slow cooker or warming tray helps) and cold food at 40°F or colder (set dishes in bowls of ice).
  • Serve on several small platters. When one empties, swap in a fresh one from the kitchen rather than adding food to a platter that’s been out.

While baking (FDA)

Don’t eat or taste raw cake batter or cookie dough, and don’t let kids lick the spoon: raw flour and raw eggs can carry germs. The same goes for using raw dough as play clay.

Cooking temperatures (USDA)

  • Chicken tenders: 165°F.
  • Hot dogs, and leftovers reheated: 165°F, steaming hot.
  • Burgers: 160°F.

Little guests

For toddlers and preschoolers, cut hot dogs, grapes and cherry tomatoes into small pieces; whole, they are choking hazards for young children (CDC). Have kids sit down to eat.

After the party

Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, in shallow containers, and eat them within 3 to 4 days. A cake with cream cheese frosting goes in the refrigerator.

📜 Cake and candles

Children’s birthday parties with a cake and candles, called Kinderfest, were a custom in Germany by the 18th century, with a candle for each year. The custom spread to Britain and America in the 1800s, as cheaper sugar, baking powder and home ovens made frosted layer cakes possible in ordinary kitchens.
