Case builder

A case is the speech you write before the round. Build it from contentions, and build each contention from points that have a claim (what you say is true), a warrant (why it's true), evidence (proof from a source) and an impact (why it matters).

Tips for a strong case

  • Two or three contentions are plenty for most constructives. A few well-proved points beat many thin ones.
  • Every piece of evidence needs a source a judge could find: the author, their qualifications, the publication and the date. Quote fairly: never cut a quotation so it says something the author didn't mean.
  • Say the impact out loud. Judges won't do the work of explaining why your point matters; tell them.
  • Read it with a timer. The estimate here uses a conversational 150 words a minute. Practice with the debate timer.
  • Prepare for the other side. Write your answers to their best arguments, not their weakest; the steelman drill helps.
