Simple scoring sheet

For practice rounds and club meetings · Teddy Rook Book

Type names and tap scores from 1 (needs work) to 5 (excellent). Totals add up as you go, and the sheet is saved in this browser. Print it filled in, or print it blank for judges to fill by hand.

Topic
SpeakerSideArgumentsEvidenceRefutationOrganizationDeliveryQuestionsTotal
What to work on
Arguments
Clear claims with reasons that link to the resolution.
Evidence
Sources that are real, relevant, fairly quoted and cited.
Refutation
Answers the other side's best points directly.
Organization
Signposts ("my first point…") and stays on time.
Delivery
Clear, audible, steady pace, eye contact, respectful tone.
Questions
Asks short, useful questions and gives truthful, direct answers.

What judges look for

  • Lay judges (parents, teachers, community members) decide like a thoughtful listener: who was clearer, more convincing and easier to follow. Speak plainly, explain jargon and slow down.
  • Flow judges (former debaters and coaches) take notes on every argument and decide on what was said and answered. A dropped argument usually counts for the side that made it, and new arguments in the last speeches usually don't count.
  • Every judge rewards honest evidence, direct answers to the other side, and a clear explanation in the last speech of why you win.
  • Write a reason for decision a debater can learn from: the argument that decided it and why, not just "good job."

Read more in Learn to Debate. For timing the round, use the debate timer.
