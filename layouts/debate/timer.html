    Using the timer

    Hand signals. Many timekeepers hold up fingers for the whole minutes left, a bent finger (or a "C" shape) at 30 seconds, and a closed fist at time. Hand-signal mode shows the same thing big on screen, so a speaker can glance at it. Check what your league expects; some use time cards instead.

    Prep time. In formats with running prep, each side has one bank of prep for the whole round. Press that side's prep button when they start preparing and again when they're ready. The speech clock pauses while prep runs. In formats where teams prepare before the round (World Schools and British Parliamentary), use the round-prep clock.

    Points of information. In formats that allow them, the clock shows when the protected time at the start and end of a speech is over and points may be offered.

    The timer keeps working without a connection once this page has loaded. It saves where you are, so a reloaded page picks up at the same speech (paused). Learn more about each format on the formats pages; for keeping notes during the round, try the flow sheet.
