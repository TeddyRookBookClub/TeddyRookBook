How to read it

  • Known spaces are passages and chambers that have been entered and measured. Only their surfaces are drawn, in meters and in royal cubits (one cubit is 0.5236 m).
  • Patterns are regularities in the surveyed numbers, such as slopes and lengths in whole cubits. A pattern is an observation about the measurements; it does not show what the builders intended.
  • Candidates are places where a space might be. Each is marked by its kind of evidence: detected by instruments (for example the “Big Void” that muon detectors found above the Grand Gallery in 2017), suggested by pattern only, or a disputed claim. None has been entered, and those suggested by pattern alone are guesses.
  • Every pyramid has a list of what is simplified or uncertain in the model, and its sources.
