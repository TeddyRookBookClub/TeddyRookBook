The rungs of the continued fraction are the best pairs of wheels: no pair with fewer teeth comes closer. Choose a row to see its wheels. A large next term marks a bargain, a small pair that is far more accurate than its size suggests.

What is known and what is ours. Christiaan Huygens chose the wheels of his planetarium this way in the 1680s, and his notes are among the first to use continued fractions for a practical problem: for Saturn he arrived at wheels of 206 and 7 teeth, from the figures of his day. The 19-year cycle of 235 months is named for Meton of Athens (432 BC), and the eclipse cycle of 223 months was known in Babylon; both are built into the gearwork of the Antikythera mechanism. The periods used here are modern values. The instrument is ours.
